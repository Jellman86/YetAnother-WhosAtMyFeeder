- A species or Garden animal is only ever described by an article about it. A Garden record of an
  Eastern Gray Squirrel showed Wikipedia's summary of a mite, because the mite's article names the
  squirrel as a host and any mention of the scientific name counted as a match. An article now has
  to have the taxon as its subject: titled with the scientific name, opening with it, or carrying
  it in the parenthetical straight after the article's own subject ("The common starling (Sturnus
  vulgaris) is…"). A host, a prey species or a member of a genus named in passing is a mention and
  is rejected, and so is a subspecies' article offered for its parent species. A renamed taxon is recognised through the synonyms the species catalogue records.
  Birds looked up by name are checked the same way whenever the taxonomy knows their scientific
  name, and a description cached before this check is validated again before it is shown, so a
  wrong one already stored is replaced rather than served.
- A record and its family tree use one name for the species. A record headed "European Starling"
  no longer ends its family tree, titles the tree dialog, or labels the selected node in the tree
  with the catalogue's "Common Starling"; other ranks and other species keep the catalogue's names.
