A small release for anyone whose Jobs view has shown "Failed to reclassify: video_worker_unavailable"
or "video_worker_heartbeat_timeout". The visit was fine. The worker analysing it had been
restarted, and the queue gave up on the visit instead of waiting.

## Smoother everyday use

- **A restarted worker no longer costs you the visit.** When the video worker is replaced
  mid-analysis, the visit goes back on the queue after a short pause and runs again on the fresh
  worker, twice at most. Its status returns to pending in the meantime, so a container restart in
  the gap picks it up like any other queued visit. Only when those attempts are spent is it marked
  failed, with the worker's reason, and a manual Reclassify then falls back to the retained
  snapshot as before.
- **Work queued during a restart waits for the new worker.** The next visit in line used to be
  handed to the worker that had just been stopped, which failed it on the spot and started a
  second replacement that was thrown away. One restart now means one restart, and the restart
  breaker is no longer charged twice for one event.
- **Video workers are judged on a budget that fits the job.** A temporal analysis is one long
  native job, so a five-second silence, the right limit for a still image, was killing healthy
  analyses. The video worker now has its own thirty-second heartbeat budget, and the video hard
  deadline still ends one that has truly hung.

## Before you update

- **No special steps.** No schema changes and no settings to revisit. Pull `latest` or the flavour
  you use as usual. Unsuffixed image tags remain the full compatibility image; see
  [hardware acceleration](../setup/hardware-acceleration.md) if you use a provider-specific
  flavour.

## Good to know

- **The new budget is a setting.** `CLASSIFICATION__VIDEO_WORKER_HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`
  defaults to `30` and accepts `1` to `300`. Leave it unless you have a reason.
- **Every worker restart is now written to the log with its evidence.** The line
  "Replacing classifier worker" carries the pool, the reason, how long the worker had been silent,
  how long it had been on the request, and the last of its own output. If a worker still restarts
  on your install, that line is what to include in an issue.
