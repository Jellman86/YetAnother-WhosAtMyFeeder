This release is mostly about a feeder that stays responsive, and a leaderboard that tells the
truth. If you have corrected a species by hand, or if the interface has ever felt like it was
waiting on something, this is the update for you.

## What's new

- **Delete a detection from "Needs your call".** Working the queue offered a species, a block, or
  hiding. Hiding keeps the record, which is right for a real bird the classifier scored badly and
  wrong for a rock. There is now a **Delete permanently** action beside the others. It asks first,
  says the record and its media leave the history for good, and reminds you that hiding keeps the
  record instead.
- **Notable nearby shows each species once.** eBird sends one report per checklist, so a rarity seen
  by four people at one reserve used to arrive as four identical cards. One card now carries the
  species, the most recent report, how many reported it, and how many places it was seen at.
- **Background work has one home.** The separate jobs view listed the same work three times over and
  named jobs after Frigate event ids. It is gone. Notifications already carried every job with its
  live progress, and the one control the jobs view held alone, resuming a paused queue, now sits at
  the top of that page. Jobs are named by what they are, with the event kept underneath.

## Smoother everyday use

- **The leaderboard lists each bird once.** Two faults did this. A species you had corrected by hand
  before 1 September kept the old bird's catalogue identity under the new name, and the leaderboard
  grouped by identity, so a Tree Sparrow you had corrected to a Dunnock counted as a second Dunnock.
  A startup pass now repairs those rows once, using the name you gave. Separately, a detection that
  had resolved its identity and one still waiting to were grouped as different lines, and the split
  grew with every new detection. Both lines now fold into one.
- **Nothing waits on the internet while holding a database connection.** Naming a species for a
  non-English reader could ask iNaturalist over the network, with a ten second timeout, while
  holding one of the five database connections everything shares. One uncached species could stall
  every other page. That lookup now answers from what it has and fetches the name in the background;
  the leaderboard and the events list give their connection back before naming anything at all.
  Measured on the reference feeder over a full day: zero waits for a connection, where the previous
  build showed thirty-one.
- **Opening a page is no longer a burst of requests.** The dashboard probed every visit on screen for
  a clip in the same second, and species pages asked for every row's photo at once. Both are now
  limited to three at a time, and the rest wait their turn. You will not see a difference in what
  loads, only that other pages stop queueing behind it.
- **A species with no Wikipedia article is remembered for a day.** It used to be searched for again
  almost every visit, and the species page could take twelve seconds for such a bird. A lookup that
  actually failed is still retried after a minute.
- **When something you did fails, the app says so.** Tagging a species, hiding a detection, running
  an AI analysis and deleting a visit used to fail silently. The worst was the delete: you confirmed
  it was permanent, it failed, and nothing changed on screen. Each now reports the failure.
- **Guests see the feeder as you configured it.** Three settings never reached a visitor: the
  review flag on low-confidence visits, the nearby-sightings window, and clip playback. They now do.

## Before you update

- **No special steps.** No schema changes and no settings to revisit. The identity repair runs once
  at startup on its own, touches only rows whose identity contradicts their own scientific name, and
  changes nothing on a second run. Unsuffixed image tags remain the full compatibility image; see
  [hardware acceleration](../setup/hardware-acceleration.md) if you use a provider-specific flavour.

## Good to know

- **The shipped model mappings were rebuilt.** 189 more model outputs resolve to a catalogue species
  than before, through matching rules rather than guesses: a hyphen or an American spelling no
  longer hides a bird, a name one model spells out in Latin now teaches the others, a subspecies
  resolves to the species IOC actually recognises, and a bird IOC has moved to another genus is
  found through the name it kept. Not one output that already resolved changed identity. The North
  American models go from 62 unresolved labels each to 4.
- **A first render of a brand-new species in a non-English language may briefly show the stored
  name.** The translation arrives in the background and the next render has it. Before, that render
  waited up to ten seconds and held everything else up while it did.
- **Model Evaluation now looks like the rest of the app.** It was the only page on a different grey,
  with buttons too small to hit comfortably on a phone. It still has no translations; that is its
  own piece of work.
